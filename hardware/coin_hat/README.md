# Coin sorter HAT+ — rev 1 sketch

KiCad 8 starting point for a Pi 5 HAT+ that carries the whole motion side of the
sorter: 12 V in, buck to 5 V for the Pi, a Raspberry Pi Pico, two StepStick
sockets (TMC2209 or A4988, selected by solder jumpers) for belt and feeder, the
MG996R diverter servo on its own buck, switched 12 V outputs, and the small
stuff (LED ring level shifter, feeder home switch, coin sensor, HAT+ ID EEPROM).

The board runs **lengthwise** along the Pi: 109 × 56.5 mm, the same width as the
Pi. The HAT+ holes and header sit at one end, and a 44 mm overhang runs past the
Pi's SD-card / PCIe end, so the drivers, jack and bucks sit over nothing. The
Pi + HAT stack is 129 × 56.5 mm, a slim box instead of rev 0's 85 × 90. The
overhang goes off the SD end, not the USB end: at 16 mm spacer height, a board
over the USB/Ethernet jacks would sit on top of the plugs.

Generated by a script, so it is a *netlist with symbols placed on a sheet*, not
a drawn schematic: every pin carries a global label and the parts are grouped by
function. Redraw with wires or leave it — KiCad is happy either way.

## Rev 1 changes (2026-09-27)

- **Diverter is the MG996R servo, not a stepper.** U4 socket, its jumpers
  JP41–44, the 4-pin motor connector and C5 are gone, and so is the diverter
  home switch (J8), because the servo is absolute. New parts: U8 is a separate
  12 V → 5–6 V buck just for the servo (+SERVO). J4 is a 3-pin 0.1" servo
  header (1 SIG, 2 V+, 3 GND). C6 is 1000 µF placed *at* J4. D5 lights when the
  servo rail is live, and GP27 reads the servo rail through a 10k/10k divider.
  GP6 drives the signal pin directly, with no series resistor and no pulldown.
  That is how the bench build works.
- **Why the LED and the sense line:** if the servo rail is dead and GP6 is
  pulsing, the servo back-powers through its signal pin. It twitches and never
  moves, which looks exactly like a signal fault. Check D5 (or GP27) first.
- **Outline goes lengthwise** (see above), with the **CSI/DSI FPC notch**
  (10 × 16.5 mm on the bottom edge, per the HAT+ spec's M.2 HAT+ drawing). Rev 0
  had no notch and would have blocked the camera cable.
- **Header position fixed.** Rev 0 had it 1.27 mm off in both axes. HAT+ spec:
  the header centre is 29 mm from the left hole, on the hole line, so pin 1 is
  at (8.37, 4.77) from the Pi corner and pin 2 is at (8.37, 2.23).

## Pin map (Pico)

| Function | Pins | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pi link | GP0 TX / GP1 RX (UART0) | Pi GPIO14/15, `/dev/ttyAMA0`, `dtparam=uart0=on`. REPL stays on USB. |
| Belt | STEP GP2, DIR GP3, EN GP4 | unchanged from `firmware/main.py`; TMC UART addr 0 |
| Diverter servo | GP6 → J4 signal | unchanged from `firmware/main.py`; direct, no resistors |
| Servo rail sense | GP27 (ADC1) | 10k / 10k: 5.4 V → 2.7 V, 6 V → 3.0 V |
| Feeder | STEP GP18, DIR GP19, EN GP22 | TMC UART addr 1 |
| TMC UART | GP20 TX via 1 k, GP21 RX | single-wire bus shared by both drivers |
| Feeder home | GP11 | to GND, internal pull-up |
| Coin sensor | GP5 | 3-pin XH |
| Key light / aux MOSFET | GP14 / GP15 | AO3400, flyback diode, 2-pin terminals |
| LED ring | GP16 → 74AHCT1G125 → 5 V data | 3-pin XH |
| VMOT sense | GP28 | 100 k / 15 k: 12 V → 1.57 V |
| Aux header | GP7, GP9, GP17, GP12/13 (I²C), GP26 (ADC0) | plus 3V3/GND |
| Spare | GP8, GP10 | no-connect |

The Pi resets the Pico by driving **GPIO17 high**, which makes Q3 pull RUN low.
It is active-high on purpose: GPIO17 defaults to pull-down at boot, so an
unconfigured Pi leaves the Pico running. `pinctrl set 17 op dh; sleep 0.1;
pinctrl set 17 dl` replaces the USB DTR toggle that relaunches `main.py` today.

## Power

Jack → F1 (mini blade) → +12 V bus. The bus feeds:

- the StepSticks (100 µF each) and the switched outputs;
- U5 (12 → 5 V, 5 A module) → +5 V → Pi header pins 2/4, and Pico VSYS
  through D1;
- U8 (12 → 5–6 V, ~3 A module) → +SERVO → J4.

Pololu D36V28F5 (5 V) or D36V28F6 (6 V) fits U8's 5-pin footprint. The MG996R
is rated 4.8–7.2 V, and 6 V gives more torque than the 5.4 V bench supply.

Feeding a Pi 5 over the header skips USB-PD, so set `usb_max_current_enable=1`
if USB peripherals need more than 600 mA. Size F1 to the real load: Pi 5 up to
~2.3 A at 12 V, plus two steppers, plus the servo (1.4 A stall at 6 V ≈ 0.8 A
at 12 V).

## Mechanical

- 16 mm board-to-board spacers (HAT+ spec recommendation for the Active
  Cooler) and a matching stacking header. Keep the bottom side clear over
  the Pi (Pico zone) and over the Pi 5 PoE header, which is marked on the
  board.
- The Pi 5 PCIe FFC connector sits under the overhang near the Pi edge. It is
  unused here, and a flat cable would still pass under a 16 mm-high board.
- The zones in the overhang are a starting split. The motor, servo and jack
  connectors go toward the left end so a case can have one connector wall.
  The 44 mm overhang is sized to rev 0's area; shrink it once parts are placed.

## Verify before ordering

- TMC2209 breakout pin order (the 16-pin socket varies between makers; DIAG /
  INDEX are often extra pins outside it).
- Solder jumpers per driver socket (JPx1–JPx4) match the driver you fit:
  **TMC2209** — JPx3=A (UART on pin 4), JPx4=A (CLK→GND), JPx1/JPx2 = UART
  address (belt 00, feeder 10). **A4988** — JPx3=B (MS3 high), JPx4=B (RESET
  tied to SLEEP), JPx1+JPx2 closed = 1/16 step; set Vref = I × 8 × Rsense
  (0.40 V for 0.5 A with R100 sense resistors); heatsink.
- Buck module pin order — U5 and U8 are drawn as generic 5-pin headers
  (VIN, GND, VOUT, SHDN, PG). Check against the Pololu pinout.
- J4 pin order vs your servo plug (usual order: signal, V+, GND; brown wire =
  GND).
- Header, holes and FPC notch against the official Pi 5 mechanical drawing. The
  socket goes on the **bottom** side.
- Belt motor current rating (TMC2209 tops out around 2 A RMS).

## Firmware implications

The feeder moves to DIR/EN on GP19/GP22, and the Pi link moves from USB CDC to
UART0. Current and hold settings move from DIP switches to TMC UART writes. The
servo stays on GP6 exactly as `firmware/main.py` drives it today; optionally
read GP27 and refuse to pulse GP6 while the servo rail is down. None of this
changes the serial protocol the Pi speaks.
